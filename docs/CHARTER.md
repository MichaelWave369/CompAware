# CompAware Research Charter
**Version 0.1 · Proposed · Open to revision**

## Mission
Study whether **Computational Awareness** can be usefully defined and investigated as a distinct family of measurable computational processes, without requiring biological imitation.

We seek concrete accounts of how a system represents its internal operational state, the external environment, boundaries, history and the consequences of its own actions.

The term is **provisional**. It is a valid outcome to conclude that its proposed components do not support a coherent distinct construct.

## Not our mission
- Proving human or machine consciousness, a soul, sentience, or subjective experience.
- Certifying rights, personhood or moral status.
- Producing humanlike language as evidence of inner awareness.
- Assuming silicon is the only possible nonbiological computational substrate.
- Assuming any preferred architecture must produce the outcome we want.

**We do not ask silicon to become carbon.** Biological consciousness is not the test standard. Computational capacities are analyzed on their own terms.

## Working principles
1. **Observe first:** distinguish implementation, measured behavior, inference, and speculation.
2. **Specificity:** each capacity has a clear operational definition and a falsifiable prediction.
3. **Adversarial challenge:** include simple controllers, scripted imitation, and module ablations.
4. **Reproducibility:** publish seeds, versions, environments, resource budgets and negative results.
5. **Uncertainty:** a functional capacity is not proof of subjective experience.
6. **Safe execution:** sandbox simulations by default, no unbounded permissions or self-protection goals.
7. **Open governance:** humans and AI-assisted collaborators may submit ideas, but every claim needs human-reviewed evidence.

## Versioning
Changes to scope, definitions or methodology belong in reviewed pull requests. Publish disagreements and disconfirming observations.

> **Founding principle:** Define nothing by imitation. Discover through observation.
